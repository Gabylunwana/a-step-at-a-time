# Programme overview

## Model

*One Step at a Time* follows the design of WHO's Step-by-Step: a minimally guided, five-session self-help programme built around **behavioural activation**, with supporting techniques — psychoeducation, slow breathing, identifying strengths, positive self-talk, increasing social support and relapse prevention. Content is delivered through an illustrated **narrative**: a person going through low mood (the reader picks Amara or Chidi) is coached by a trusted figure (Ada, a community health worker). The reader learns alongside the character, then practises the same skill in an interactive exercise.

Why behavioural activation at the centre: it is simple, can be taught by non-specialists, and is among the most effective psychological approaches for depression. The other techniques support it — slow breathing helps with anxiety before hard activities; social contact is itself activation; kinder self-talk counters the thoughts that stop people acting.

## Session flow

Every session follows the same rhythm, so readers know what to expect:

1. **Check-in** — mood, daily functioning, stress (0–10) + safety question
2. **Review** — how did last week's planned activities go? (Sessions 2–5)
3. **Story** — the companion's experience and the guide's teaching
4. **Skill** — short information page
5. **Practice** — an interactive exercise
6. **Plan** — activities for the coming week
7. **Summary** — key points + what to practise

## Sessions

### 0 · Getting started (~15 min)
Welcome, limits of self-help, privacy, urgent help. Choose companion. First check-in. *Exercises:* main problems (3), strengths checklist.

### 1 · Understanding how you feel
Signs of low mood; normalising. **The low-mood cycle** (diagram). "Action comes first, motivation follows." Enjoyable vs. getting-things-done activities. Small, specific, timed plans. *Exercises:* past activities checklist; plan 2–3 activities.

### 2 · Managing stress and doing more
Review: partial success reframed as information. **Breaking big tasks into steps.** Body's stress response; **slow breathing** (in 4, out 6, ~2 min) with an animated timer. *Exercises:* task breakdown; guided breathing; calm rating; new plan.

### 3 · Kinder thoughts and your strengths
Harsh inner voice; four common unhelpful patterns. "Would you say that to a friend?" **Positive self-talk** (not pretending — being fair). Revisit strengths via past coping. *Exercises:* harsh→kinder thought pairs; times I got through; personal kind sentence; plan.

### 4 · Connecting with others
Withdrawal and feeling a burden. **Four kinds of support** (company, listening, practical, advice) and sources. Reconnecting step by step; a short message is enough. *Exercises:* people/places list; one connection step + wording; plan.

### 5 · Staying well
Progress chart of check-ins. Toolbox recap. Ups and downs are normal. **Relapse prevention:** warning signs, actions, contacts; when to seek professional help. *Exercises:* tools that helped; warning signs; action plan; contacts; hopes.

## Measures

The built-in check-in is a brief, non-diagnostic self-rating designed for the reader's own tracking. If you are running a service or evaluation, add validated measures outside the app (for example a depression symptom questionnaire and a functioning scale chosen with your clinical lead), administered by helpers at baseline and end.
