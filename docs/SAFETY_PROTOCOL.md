## Helper steps when risk is disclosed

1. **Stay calm and keep talking.** Thank them for telling you. Don't end the call abruptly.
2. **Ask directly** (it does not increase risk):
   - "Are you thinking about ending your life?"
   - "Have you made a plan? Do you have the means?"
   - "Have you ever tried before?"
   - "Is anyone with you right now?"
3. **Decide the level:**
   - **Imminent** — has a plan + means + intent, or has already acted → contact emergency services / arrange immediate transfer to care; stay on the line; inform supervisor immediately.
   - **High** — frequent thoughts, some planning, cannot commit to safety → same-day professional assessment; contact supervisor during the call; agree who will be with them.
   - **Lower** — occasional thoughts, no plan, can identify reasons for living and supports → make a safety plan together (warning signs, coping steps, people to contact, crisis numbers, removing means), encourage seeing a health worker, follow up within 24–72 hours, inform supervisor.
4. **Record** what was said and done, and the follow-up plan.
5. **Pause the programme** for anyone at imminent or high risk until a professional has reviewed them.

## Other situations needing referral

Psychotic symptoms; severe depression with inability to function; risk of harming others; ongoing abuse or violence (follow local safeguarding procedures); harmful alcohol or drug use; a participant under 18.

## Helper wellbeing

Risk calls are stressful. Debrief with your supervisor the same day.
